# 02 — User Personas

## Primary: "Planning Priya" — the researching parent

- Parent of a 2–5 year old, active on Instagram/Pinterest, follows
  parenting/Montessori accounts.
- Screen-time-conscious; actively looking for offline, hands-on
  learning activities.
- Compares 3–4 options before buying; reads About/testimonials before
  trusting a small/independent brand.
- Buys on mobile, often during a scroll break (evening, commute).
- **Needs from the site**: fast trust signals (real photos, warm
  testimonials), clear age-appropriateness per workbook, an
  effortless way to ask a question before buying (hence the floating
  WhatsApp button).

## Secondary: "Gifting Grandparent / Relative"

- Less tech-fluent, arrives via a shared link (WhatsApp/Instagram),
  not organic search.
- Wants to buy 1–2 items quickly as a gift, doesn't want to create an
  account or navigate a complex flow.
- **Needs from the site**: extremely simple add-to-cart → WhatsApp
  checkout, no login walls, large tappable targets, clear pricing.

## Tertiary: "Bulk-Buying Educator/Playschool Owner"

- Runs a small preschool or activity class, wants multiple copies of
  the same workbook.
- Price-sensitive, wants to negotiate quantity — WhatsApp checkout
  actually suits this persona well since a human can adjust pricing in
  chat.
- **Needs from the site**: easy quantity adjustment in cart, clear
  total before handoff to WhatsApp.

## The child (indirect user)

- Toddlers won't browse the site, but the **hero imagery and product
  photography** need to visually signal "made for me" — bright,
  friendly, illustrated/photographed workbook spreads, not just plain
  product shots on white.

## Design implications

- Every persona converts through the same shallow funnel: Home/Shop →
  Add to Cart → WhatsApp. Don't add steps (accounts, multi-page
  checkout) that only make sense for a payment-gateway flow.
- Trust-building content (About, Testimonials) must be reachable from
  the homepage without hunting — these personas decide fast.
